# Consciousness Under Correction

**Subtitle:** Structural Intelligence, Warrant, Fixed Worth, and the Architecture of Answerable Intelligence  
**Author:** Vladisav Jovanović  
**Status:** Preprint / theoretical synthesis  
**Version:** Readable Complete Edition (August 2026)

### Abstract

This paper presents Structural Intelligence as a theory of correctability. People, theories, relationships, institutions, and artificial systems all require enough stability to preserve memory, identity, action, coordination, or explanation. The problem begins when a useful organizing structure protects its own coherence against evidence, consequence, other perspectives, or practical failure.

The framework separates discrepancy, contact, correction claim, warrant, response, binding, and continued revisability. Warrant is evidential, procedural, and proportional. This makes it possible to distinguish calibrated revision from defensive closure, reasoned resistance, compliance, suspended judgment, and performative revision. Correction-capacity is defined as warrant-response calibration over time: justified correction should change later confidence, behavior, repair, decision pathways, burden allocation, or evidential practice, while weak or coercive correction should be resisted.

For human cases, the paper develops fixed worth and person-belief differentiation as possible conditions that make self-relevant correction more survivable. It also distinguishes observation, attention, witness, and answerable witness, and introduces the mastery risk: a theory of correction fails when it uses correction-language to control reality rather than remain correctable by reality. The framework is extended to psyche, relationships, institutions, and AI while keeping domain mechanisms separate.

For AI, the paper distinguishes fluent self-correction, persistent memory, corrective continuity, perceived social presence, and phenomenal consciousness. Corrective continuity means trace-bearing revision that persists across relevant contexts and remains re-revisable; it does not establish sentience. The paper also develops explicit falsifiers, a research program, and a compact operational index. Its central claim is that intelligence is better revealed by reorganization under warranted correction than by coherence, confidence, or polished self-consistency alone.
